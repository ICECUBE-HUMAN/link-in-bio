A link in bio page and a personal website solve related problems, but they are not the same thing. The right choice depends on how much content you need and how quickly you want to publish.

## When a link in bio page is enough

Choose a link in bio page when your main goal is to connect a social audience to a few important destinations. It is a good fit when you need:

- one short URL for every social profile
- a clear list of your latest work and key links
- a simple public page that works well on a phone
- a page you can update without maintaining a full website
- a small amount of text, media, and location information around your links

For a creator, freelancer, or small business, this can be the fastest way to create a useful online home.

## When a personal website makes sense

A full website is useful when you need multiple pages, long-form content, a detailed portfolio, a store with a larger catalog, or more control over navigation and layout. It gives you room for deeper information and more complex flows.

That extra control also brings more work: choosing a platform, managing pages, maintaining forms, and keeping the site fast and current.

## You can use both

The decision does not have to be permanent. Many people use a link in bio page as the front door and link to their personal website from it. Social visitors get a focused first screen, while people who want more detail can continue to the full site.

The important thing is to keep the path clear. Put the personal website near the top when it is your main destination, and use the surrounding links to answer common questions before a visitor leaves.

## A practical starting point

If you are still deciding, begin with the smallest page that supports your current goal. Write your name and purpose, add your primary link, then include two or three supporting destinations. You can always expand later when visitors have new questions.

[Create a focused public page with Grabbin](/) and combine links with media, text, and favorite places. If your needs grow into a larger website, the page can continue to serve as the simple home your social audience already knows.

The best tool is the one you will keep updated. A small, current page is more useful than a large website that visitors cannot navigate.
