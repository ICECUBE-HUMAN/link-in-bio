Your Instagram profile is often the first place people look after discovering you. The challenge is that your audience may need more than one destination: your latest work, portfolio, shop, booking page, or another social profile.

Instagram’s built-in link options can be enough for a short list. When you need a clearer layout or more context around each destination, a link in bio page gives you one stable URL to place in your profile.

## Option 1: Use Instagram’s built-in links

If you only need a few destinations, add them directly to your Instagram profile:

1. Open your Instagram profile.
2. Tap **Edit profile**.
3. Open the links section.
4. Add each external URL and its title.
5. Save, then open your profile as a visitor to check the result.

The exact labels and limits can change as Instagram updates its profile tools. The important check is whether a visitor can quickly understand which link to tap.

## Option 2: Use one link in bio page

A dedicated page is helpful when your links need more organization. You can place one URL in Instagram and let that page hold the rest of your destinations.

This approach is useful when you want to:

- put one campaign or new release at the top
- explain what each link is before someone taps it
- include media or text between groups of links
- share a location or favorite place alongside your other work
- keep the same Instagram URL while your destinations change

Create your page with [Grabbin](/), choose a memorable handle, and add only the links that support your current goal.

## A simple structure that works

Use this order as a starting point:

1. **Profile:** name, photo, and a one-line description.
2. **Primary action:** the one thing you want most visitors to do.
3. **Recent work:** your latest article, video, release, or project.
4. **Evergreen links:** portfolio, shop, booking, or contact.
5. **Social links:** places where people can keep following you.

Give links specific labels. “My website” is less useful than “See the spring collection” or “Book a consultation.” Clear labels set an expectation before the click.

## Check the page on mobile

Most Instagram visitors will see your page on a phone. Test the first screen without scrolling. Your name, purpose, and primary link should be visible immediately. Tap every link, check that external pages open correctly, and remove anything that is outdated.

## Keep one URL, change the content

The biggest benefit of a link in bio page is continuity. You can change the page as your work changes without updating every place where you shared the profile URL. Review the page whenever you launch something new, and move the current priority to the top.
