When someone says “link in bio,” they usually mean one short URL in a social profile that opens a page with several destinations. Instead of choosing between your latest video, portfolio, shop, newsletter, or contact page, you give people one clear place to start.

## Why do people use a link in bio?

Social platforms are excellent for discovery, but each platform gives you a different amount of space for external links. A link in bio page gives your audience a consistent home base that you can share from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, or anywhere else.

It is useful for:

- creators sharing their latest work and social profiles
- freelancers showing a portfolio and a way to get in touch
- small businesses sharing a menu, location, booking page, and store
- musicians collecting streaming links, tour dates, and merchandise
- anyone who wants a simple public page instead of a full website

## How a link in bio page works

The idea is simple:

1. Create a public page with a unique handle.
2. Add your most important links and supporting content.
3. Copy the page URL into your social profile.
4. Send followers there when you publish something new.

The page becomes a small, focused directory. You can update the destinations without changing the URL in every social profile.

## What should you include?

Start with the information your visitor is most likely looking for. A useful page normally includes:

- your name, project, or business name
- one sentence explaining what you do
- a primary link for the action you care about most
- your latest work or announcement
- social profiles that help people follow you elsewhere
- a location, contact page, or booking link when it matters

Avoid adding every link you have ever created. A shorter page is easier to scan, especially on a phone. Put the most important destination first, then group the rest by purpose.

## The short version

A link in bio is more than a list of URLs. It is a compact public page that connects your social audience to the rest of your work. Keep it clear, update it when your priorities change, and make the first tap feel easy.
