A link in bio page can be more than a handoff from social media. When it has a public, readable URL and useful text, it can also help people discover your name, work, or business through search.

Search visibility is never guaranteed. Search engines decide which pages to show and how to describe them. You can still make the page easier to understand by taking care of the basics.

## 1. Use a recognizable page name

Choose a handle that is close to your name, project, or business. A short, memorable URL is easier to share and easier for people to recognize when it appears in a message or search result.

Avoid changing the handle often. Consistency helps your audience and reduces the number of old URLs you need to explain.

## 2. Write a specific bio

Your name and bio are important context. Say what you do, who you help, or what kind of work people will find on the page. Include a natural phrase people might use when looking for you, but write for humans first.

Weak: “Welcome to my page.”

Stronger: “Independent product designer sharing interface experiments, case studies, and practical design notes.”

## 3. Give links descriptive labels

Search engines and visitors both understand “Read my accessibility checklist” faster than “Click here.” Descriptive labels also make the page useful to people who scan quickly or use assistive technology.

## 4. Add real supporting content

A page made only of anonymous URLs has little context. Add short text, recent media, or a favorite place when it helps explain your work. Keep the content original and useful; repeating the same keyword many times can make the page harder to read.

## 5. Keep the page public and current

Remove expired links, fix broken destinations, and keep your main action near the top. A page that looks abandoned is less helpful to visitors and less likely to earn continued attention.

## 6. Connect the page from your other profiles

Use the same page URL on the social profiles where people already know you. Mentions from your own channels make the page easier for followers to find and give search engines a clear connection between your identity and the public page.
