More clicks do not come from adding more links. They come from reducing uncertainty. When a visitor knows who you are, what they will find, and what to do next, the page becomes much easier to use.

## 1. Choose one primary action

Decide what success means for this week or month. It might be a new video, a product, a booking request, or your portfolio. Put that destination first and give it a specific label.

“Latest video: how I built my studio” tells people much more than “YouTube.” The label should explain the value of the click without making the visitor guess.

## 2. Write a useful bio

Your bio should answer three questions:

- Who are you?
- What do you make or offer?
- Why should someone keep exploring?

Use plain language and remove filler. “Illustrator sharing practical drawing lessons” is easier to understand than a long list of vague descriptions. A clear bio also gives search engines useful context for a public page.

## 3. Put the important links first

People rarely need to see every destination before deciding what to do. Put your primary link near the top, then group supporting links below it. If a link is important enough to promote, it should not be hidden at the bottom of a long list.

## 4. Use sections to reduce scanning effort

Separate recent work, services, social profiles, and places when the page contains several types of information. A short heading can help someone jump to the right group without reading every label.

## 5. Keep labels specific

Good labels describe an action or outcome:

- View my portfolio
- Book a 30-minute call
- Read the new article
- Find me at the studio

Specific labels build trust because the visitor knows what will happen after the tap.

## 6. Design for the first screen

Open the page on a phone and look at the first screen without scrolling. Your identity, purpose, and main link should be clear immediately. Keep decorative elements from pushing the useful information too far down.

## 7. Review the page after every launch

A link in bio page can become stale quickly. Move the current campaign to the top, remove expired announcements, and check each external URL. A five-minute review keeps the page more helpful than a large redesign done once a year.
