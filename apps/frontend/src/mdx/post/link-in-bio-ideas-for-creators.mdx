A good link in bio page feels like a small, well-organized home page. It should help a new visitor understand what you make and help a returning follower find your newest work without hunting.

Here are 15 ideas you can adapt to your own page.

## Content and community

1. **Your latest project:** put the newest release, article, or video first.
2. **A work archive:** collect your strongest work in one place.
3. **Your portfolio:** show the projects that explain your skills best.
4. **A behind-the-scenes page:** share process notes, sketches, or making-of content.
5. **Your newsletter or community:** make it easy for people to stay connected.

## Work and business

6. **A booking link:** help clients schedule the next conversation.
7. **A contact page:** provide one clear way to ask about a project.
8. **A service menu:** explain what you offer before asking people to enquire.
9. **A shop or product page:** send visitors directly to the item you are promoting.
10. **A media kit:** give collaborators the important facts about your work.

## Identity and discovery

11. **Your other social profiles:** help people find the platform they prefer.
12. **A short introduction:** say who you help, what you make, or where you work.
13. **Your location:** add a favorite place, studio, venue, or service area.
14. **A frequently asked question:** answer the question new visitors ask most.
15. **A current announcement:** make an event, launch, or deadline impossible to miss.

## How to choose what belongs on the page

Do not add all 15 ideas at once. Pick one main goal and two or three supporting links. For example, a photographer might lead with recent work, then add portfolio, booking, and Instagram. A local café might lead with the menu, then add location, hours, and reviews.

The best link in bio page is not the one with the most buttons. It is the one that answers a visitor’s next question quickly.

## Make the page feel like yours

Use the same name, tone, and visual choices that people already recognize from your content. Add a short bio that says something useful rather than repeating your username. Give each link a label that explains the outcome.

With [Grabbin](/), you can combine links with media, text, and favorite places so the page feels like a personal page rather than a plain directory. Rearrange the page when your most important story changes.

## A quick review checklist

- Can a new visitor tell who you are in five seconds?
- Is the main action at the top?
- Do the labels describe the destination clearly?
- Are old campaigns and broken links removed?
- Does the page look good on a small phone screen?

Start small, review the page after each launch, and let your current goal decide what stays visible.
